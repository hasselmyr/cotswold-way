This is not a path

There is no waymarked trail and there are no signposts. The line here runs between named tors across open blanket bog, tussock and peat hag. Reckon on eleven Dartmoor miles costing what sixteen Cotswold miles cost, and treat map and compass as a requirement, not a nicety — the north moor in cloud is genuinely featureless. Carry OS Explorer OL28.

Distances on this page are measured straight-line between features. Real walking adds roughly a fifth, which is why 29 line-miles is about 35 on the ground.

Live firing — check before you book

This eastern line crosses Okehampton Range between miles 21.6 and 25.3, which I measured against the mapped danger area. It deliberately avoids Merrivale and Willsworthy, the two ranges that fire heavily; in the 31 August to 11 October 2026 programme those fired on 16 and 17 dates while Okehampton fired on two: 6 and 7 October.

Check gov.uk “Dartmoor firing times” when you pick dates and again the week before. Free line 0800 458 4868. If the red flags are not flying by 9am there is no firing that day.

Why you can camp here

Dartmoor Commons Act 1985, section 10(1) gives a right of access to the commons on foot for open-air recreation. In Darwall v Dartmoor National Park Authority [2025] UKSC 20, decided 21 May 2025, the Supreme Court held unanimously that camping is open-air recreation within that right.

Two limits. The right covers the commons, about 359 km², not the whole park at about 953 km². And it is subject to byelaws, so the Authority’s published map governs where you actually pitch: 28,272 hectares across 70 polygons, which I pulled from their own feature service. The green ground on the map below is that data, not an approximation.

The rules: two nights maximum in one spot, groups of six maximum, small tents out of sight of roads and houses, at least 100 m from any road, no fires ever, bury waste 15 cm down. The layer is dated 22 March 2024, which predates the judgment, and the byelaw review is paused — so check the live map the week you go rather than trusting this copy.

Getting there and back

No bus at any point

Out

Bristol Temple Meads to Ivybridge direct on the GWR Plymouth service, about 2h15. On a Sunday the 07:54 arriving 10:11 is the only direct one; miss it and you change at Exeter St Davids.

Back

Okehampton on the Dartmoor Line, hourly, seven days a week, about 40 minutes to Exeter St Davids and roughly 2h15 home. Use Okehampton station, not Okehampton Interchange — the Interchange is 2.2 km east by the A30, the old station is on the moor side of town.

The night before

Harford Bunkhouse and Camping sits at the moor gate, two miles above Ivybridge. Forty tent pitches and an eight-berth bunkhouse. 01752 691883. Going down on the Friday evening buys you full daylight on day one.

Food

One stop in 29 miles: the East Dart Inn at Postbridge, mile 17. Ring ahead. Carry everything else. Water is everywhere but peat-stained — filter it.

Bail-out

Postbridge at mile 17 is the only road crossing with a bus, and it is sparse. Realistically the escapes are back the way you came or on to Okehampton. This is the trade for having no bus in the plan.

Season

Go before 25 October. Daylight falls from 12.8 hours in mid-September to 10.2 by then, and the Sunday train does not put you on the moor until midday. After heavy rain the north moor becomes a swamp and the river crossings get serious.

Before you go

  • Check the firing programme for your dates. gov.uk “Dartmoor firing times”, or 0800 458 4868. Weekends are almost always clear.
  • Check the live camping map at dartmoor.gov.uk and clear your cache. The data behind this page is dated 22 March 2024 and the byelaw review is paused, so it may move.
  • Confirm somebody can navigate on a compass in cloud. If not, do the 17-mile north moor horseshoe from Okehampton instead — one station, both ends, and you can bail to it in half a day from anywhere.
  • Book Harford for the Friday night. 01752 691883
  • Ring the East Dart Inn at Postbridge. It is the only food in 29 miles.
  • Carry a trowel. Bury waste 15 cm down. No fires, ever — this is the condition the right depends on.
