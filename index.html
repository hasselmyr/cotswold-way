Camping the Cotswold Way

Forty-seven miles down the escarpment from Stonehouse to Bath Abbey, split into four days with three nights on campsites. Three days of about thirteen miles, then a short morning into Bath.

Emergency closure near Wortley — check before booking

National Trails posted an emergency closure order for a section near Wortley on 3 September 2026, after drought and high winds. Wortley is at mile 28, on day two between Wotton-under-Edge and Hawkesbury Upton. The notice says a diversion will be in place and that details are to follow; nothing has been published yet.

Ring the trail team on 07841 663607 or email cotswoldway@cotswolds-nl.org.uk. Two other diversions are listed on the same stretch: a permanent one at North Nibley and an ongoing one at Beacon Lane Plantation, Tormarton.

The four days

Distances along the real trail line
1

Stonehouse to North Nibley

13.0 mi+597 m−517 m6–7 hrs

The hardest day of the four. The path climbs onto the escarpment above King’s Stanley and follows the edge south over Coaley Peak and Uley Bury. Coaley Peak is 348 ft spread over 1.2 miles of shaded woodland track, so it is easier than the profile suggests. The descent off Uley Bury is 271 ft at about 18% and is slippery in the wet. Cam Long Down is 207 ft in a fifth of a mile. The climb out of Dursley onto Stinchcombe Hill is 421 ft, with a 325 ft pitch at about 17%.

Stinchcombe Hill
At the top of the Dursley climb the path splits and both branches are signed Cotswold Way. The perimeter loop is 2.8 miles of level plateau; the by-pass saves 2.6 miles and no climbing. Drakestone Point, on the loop, is the best viewpoint on this half of the trail.
Food and water
Dursley at mile 8 is the last large shop before Bath. Taps at Selsley church and North Nibley.
Night 1
Hunts Court, North Nibley. 500 m off the path, £12 per person, hot shower and outdoor kitchen. Four pitches only. 01453 544632
2

North Nibley to Horton

12.8 mi+457 m−427 m6 hrs

The day opens with the climb to the Tyndale Monument, 427 ft over a mile with the last stretch at 14%. The tower is free and open daily, 120 steps to a gallery. The path then drops into Wotton-under-Edge and climbs 459 ft in eight-tenths of a mile back out of it, which is the longest continuous climb on this half of the trail. After that the going is level and quick through Alderley, Hawkesbury Upton and Horton.

The closure
Wortley is about four miles into this day, just south of Wotton. This is the leg affected by the emergency closure order.
Food and water
Co-op in Wotton to 22:00. The Beaufort Arms at Hawkesbury Upton serves lunch every day 12:00–14:30, the only pub south of Dursley that does; the village stores open at 07:00 daily. Taps at Wotton church porch and Alderley churchyard.
Night 2
Starvale Farm 07786 962913 or Hideaway Horton 07808 912788, both at Horton on the National Trail list. Neither has a website. Fallback 2.5 miles earlier: France Lane Farm, Hawkesbury Upton, 01454 238215
3

Horton to Lansdown

13.7 mi+426 m−379 m6–7 hrs

The fastest ground on the route. Rolling arable past Old Sodbury hillfort to Tormarton, then parkland around the wall of Dyrham Park, up to Cold Ashton and onto the Lansdown ridge. One sustained climb of 412 ft at about 9%. Once on the ridge it is level but fully exposed, with no shade or shelter for three miles.

Carry lunch and water
Old Sodbury to Lansdown is 11.4 miles with nothing on it. The Dyrham Park tea room turns away non-members and entry is £23–25. Cold Ashton has no shop, pub or café. The one reliable stop is the Londis at Pennsylvania service station on the A46, open 06:00–22:00 daily. Fill bottles at the Tormarton church tap.
Night 3
Lansdown Campsite, on the route at mile 7.9, 50 grass pitches. 07534 894999 The tent field is described in several places as summer-only, so confirm your dates. The Charlcombe Inn is 500 m away for dinner.
If Lansdown is shut
Cleeve Hill (mile 3.1) or Penn Hill (mile 2.9) make this day 18.4 miles and the last one a two-mile walk.
4

Lansdown into Bath

7.9 mi+180 m−345 m3–4 hrs

Three miles of level ridge past the Little Down promontory fort, then the path drops off the edge. Prospect Stile at 4.6 miles out is the first view of Bath, and is now a kissing gate rather than a stile. The route passes Kelston Round Hill, goes along the front of the Royal Crescent and ends at the Abbey. Bath Spa station is a third of a mile further on.

Why the short last day
Finishing before lunch means no one is packing a wet tent against a train time, and there is an afternoon in Bath at the end of it.

Campsites

Highlighted rows are the three nights

Wild camping

Wild camping is not lawful on the Cotswold Way. The National Trail says so directly, and pitching without the landowner’s permission is civil trespass in England. The open commons do not change that: Selsley Common, Stinchcombe Hill and Lansdown are access land, but the CRoW Act grants access on foot and its Schedule 2 excludes camping, including staying overnight or putting up a tent. Dartmoor is the only exception in England, and that applies to the Dartmoor Commons alone.

On price, none of the sites below comes close to £30. Hunts Court is £12 a head, France Lane about the same, Ashen Plains around £20 for a pitch rather than per person, and the Compass Inn takes tents for a donation. Three nights works out at roughly £35–50 each with showers. The lawful version of a wild pitch is asking the farmer, which is how it is usually done on this stretch.

Checked and ruled out

  • Woodside Field, Old Sodbury — motorhomes only, no tents, in their own words. Still listed as a campsite on several sites.
  • Conygres, Wotton-under-Edge — Scout site, exclusive whole-site hire only, no single pitches, no showers.
  • The Cider Orchard, North Nibley — a festival field that opens one week a year.
  • Cotswold Meadow, Chipping Sodbury — permanently closed, still listed by three booking platforms.
  • Hill Farm, Cold Ashton — no camping, and fully booked until 1 November.

Getting there and back

All trains GWR

Out

Bristol Temple Meads to Stonehouse (SHU), changing at Gloucester, 1h15–1h35, hourly Monday to Saturday. There is no direct train: Stonehouse is on the Golden Valley line, not the Bristol–Gloucester one. Fifteen minutes on foot from the station to the path at Ryeford.

Back

Bath Spa to Bristol runs every 15–30 minutes, last train about 23:40. The trail ends at the Abbey, a third of a mile from the platform.

Bail-out

Yate is the best escape on this half: bus 620 or 41 from Old Sodbury, then 20 minutes by train to Bristol. Cam & Dursley is direct from Bristol in 35 minutes. Taxi: Cam & Dursley Taxis, 07577 753530.

Sundays

The Y8, 60, 40, 620, 41 and 35 have no Sunday service, so there is effectively no bus between Dursley and Bath on a Sunday. Trains run as normal. Plan any Sunday as a day that ends at a station or in Bath.

Bag transfer

Carryabag, 01242 250642, is the only firm that lists campsites as pickup points and it covers every stop here. About £6.60 a bag for a group of three or four. Season ends 31 October, bags out by 9am.

Charfield

The new station opens in spring 2027, 32 minutes from Bristol and two miles from Wotton. It will make the shorter versions of this walk much easier, but not this year.

Three days, camping

Calls to make first

  • Trail team, about Wortley. 07841 663607. The diversion may add distance to day two.
  • Lansdown Campsite — is the tent field open on your dates? 07534 894999. If not, day three becomes 18.4 miles.
  • Hunts Court — four pitches for how many of you? 01453 544632. The tightest bottleneck on the route.
  • Horton — Starvale or Hideaway. 07786 962913 / 07808 912788. Confirm they are trading before planning a day that ends there.
